# Thanksgiving Day, after even more DFW

read/re-read a few more of his essays and shorts: “Forever Overhead” ([as read so beautifully by the man himself](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qiuz2L6Aqdc&list=WL&index=1)), “Little Expressionless Animals”, “David Lynch Keeps His Head…” I should have had time to read more, but there was the Cortázar from Ash, and this morning the Cheever, and the meals to make, and the drums to edit… but I’m coming upon a conclusion about myself that I’d rather be well-read than *widely* read, language providing a more personal, or internal, liberation than a social or *political* (in the classical sense) one; that is to say it helps me understand myself, explain myself to myself.

and indeed, reading DFW, even at a young age, even before I’d seen *Twin Peaks* or *Blue Velvet* or *Mulholland Drive*, set my crazy thoughts in order, gave them a kind of beauty, and I’m drawn to stories which promise you that, 1) all this thinking is heading somewhere, and 2) freedom lies at the end of it. such is the “liberation” (to quote myself) felt in the breathtaking endings of “Hello.” and “Not another word.” a promise that you’re stepping into something *past* all the thinking, but that the thinking is necessary to get you there at all. one feels crazy with all the thinking down there sixteen at the creek and smoking but you are stepping into the skin of your life…

I hadn’t read “Forever Overhead” in a long while. it hit hard, my Saturn having returned, cleaning up after my dinner for one in May’s beautiful apartment. the title reads differently to me now, screams almost of an unsticking-in-time, one could say I’m still that boy somewhere, I never left the creek, we never left the high dive. whereas in youth, from that high perspective you have forever before you, at some point you realize you’re in the water and you can still remember so vividly what the view was like up there, how it felt: the fear, the exhilaration. and so the whole thing becomes an exercise in empathy and remembrance. for the floppy flappy awkward boy on the rung of something Big: life. cheesy, but DFW took it seriously and gave it the beauty it deserves, it’s such a shame that he couldn’t give that grace to his own.

feeling Thankful.
