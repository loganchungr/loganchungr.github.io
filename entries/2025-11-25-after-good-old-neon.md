# after “Good Old Neon” (Oblivion, 2004)

as far as narrators who just can't get out of their own heads, this one takes the cake. I remember an afternoon talk with Drew at Wilburn St this past summer, Emory having dropped me off, having lent me her car too, god bless her. Drew made me the sandwich and we talked about his decision to leave the city and move back to Nashville. we talked too about Vonnegut and David Foster Wallace, of his deep and profound LOVE for human beings, which people tend to miss because of the outsized meme-ity of his persona online, that zombified version of his legacy which has little to do with his writing, less still with the man himself or his characters. here that humanism is on full display.

you have to surrender to your experiences and trust yourself that they are real, that your feelings are real, GOOD even, that YOU ARE GOOD, or at least TRYING TO BE GOOD, to live perhaps in a state of constant tension (at most) with that other, endlessly critical part of yourself. it will imprison you otherwise. my heart aches for Gen Z, for my own generation too, maybe for all of us younger than a certain age, because this sense of constantly being policed, of being watched, of every motive and action being circumspect and SUBJECT TO the scrutiny/judgement/scorn of others, that mechanism is externalized now, but atomized as we are through the portals of our screens, we experience the panopticon individually, and unintentionally subject one another to it continuously. it has become a collective experience of schizoid subjectivity.

how then to say together, "not another word." first by remembering the keyhole, by remembering how to open the doors, by remembering we're all dealing with an entire universe of incommunicable Stuff inside of us that aches to be seen, heard, accepted, loved. I love how he affirms forcefully that the part of us that believes in these things is the "realer" part, and that it takes "firepower" to allow it to steer the car, even though the turning on those "high beams" might in some ways simply make the "fog" that much thicker. but anyways. not another word!
